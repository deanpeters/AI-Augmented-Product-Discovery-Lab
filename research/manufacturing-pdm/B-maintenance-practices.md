# B. How manufacturing maintenance is practiced, and what unplanned downtime costs

Researched 2026-10-03. Labels: ACTUAL DATA = in a source I opened and read; INFERRED = my interpretation; UNKNOWN = not found or not verifiable. Local copies of fetched files are in this folder (om5.txt, nist.txt, tcod22.txt, tcod24.txt, deloitte.txt, cnc.txt, vb.txt).

Audience for the case: maintenance managers at mid-sized US plants deciding which equipment issue to investigate next. Most evidence below is NOT about that audience (large global firms, mixed facilities, non-US samples). That is flagged per row.

## Gaps and conflicts

Conflicts:
1. Maintenance mix baselines disagree. DOE (2010, citing a study from about 2000): over 55% reactive. Plant Engineering 2014 (Mobil-sponsored): 57% use reactive. UpKeep 2026: 30.9% mostly reactive, 43.6% balanced. MaintainX 2025: 58% spend more than half their time reacting. Fluke 2026: 36% reactive. These measure different things (share of work, share of assets, share of respondents), different populations, and different years. Do not average them.
2. "Good" reactive share is contested: DOE says top performers run under 10% reactive; NIST cites 30-40% as an ideal level in some literature.
3. Savings from PdM range from 5-10% (Deloitte) to 25% (Deloitte, same paper) to 25-30% (DOE) to 40% (Siemens, vendor deployments) to 15-98% (NIST literature range). No independent measurement spans these.
4. Siemens' own two editions revise baselines: per-plant annual cost $129 million (2022 edition) vs $253 million (2024 edition), and sample size 56 vs 181. The 2024 headline claims a fall of 6% in total loss since 2022 while per-plant cost nearly doubled in its own reports, which suggests revised methodology or sample. INFERRED, not stated by Siemens.
5. The $260,000 per hour Aberdeen figure is commonly presented as a manufacturing average but, in the whitepaper I opened, it traces to a 2016 report on IT infrastructure uptime. INFERRED mis-attribution; the original Aberdeen report was NOT OPENED.
6. MaintainX 58%: stated as "more than half reacting" in the press release and as "less than half scheduled" on its blog. Not equivalent.
7. Downtime cost per hour is not comparable across Siemens (large plant lines, four sectors), ABB (mixed process and utility sectors, $103,000 US typical), the Aberdeen IT figure, and aggregator rows for discrete manufacturing ($10,000-$50,000).

Gaps (UNKNOWN):
- No cost-of-downtime figure measured for mid-sized US discrete manufacturers. Siemens says its data mainly covers large firms; its SME line ($150,000 per hour at the top end) has no method.
- No independent (non-vendor) survey I could open with a current, manufacturing-only, US-only maintenance mix. Plant Engineering's current edition, Reliabilityweb/Uptime, LNS and Aberdeen were not opened.
- Wrench time, CMMS adoption and paper or spreadsheet use: only vendor-aggregator secondary figures surfaced. No primary sample opened. Work-order backlog: no figure.
- No meta-analysis or controlled field study of PdM impact on downtime. Evidence is vendor deployments, single case studies and cost models with assumptions.
- ScienceDirect systematic review, IEEE Access SME mapping study, ABB original page, and McKinsey originals NOT OPENED.

INFERRED implications for the case (not evidence):
- Most "savings" numbers trace back to vendor client work or untraceable older studies; treat as hypotheses to test, not as expected ROI.
- UpKeep and NIST both point to capacity, production access, cost and data readiness as the limiting factors, rather than lack of prediction. That is a hypothesis worth testing with maintenance managers: is the bottleneck knowing what will fail, or having the time and parts to act on it?
- A product team should plan to measure downtime cost with the target plants, since no published number fits their segment.
