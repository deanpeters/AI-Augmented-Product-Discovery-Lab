# E. Is information, or permission/coordination/planning/trust, the real obstacle to the maintenance investigation decision?

Researcher notes, compiled 2026-10-03. Case: AI predictive-maintenance product for mid-sized US manufacturing plants. Question: when equipment reports or alerts conflict, is poor or uncertain INFORMATION the real obstacle to what the maintenance manager investigates next, or do PERMISSION, COORDINATION, scheduling, planning and trust matter more?

Labels: ACTUAL DATA = in a source I opened and read (page text or PDF). INFERRED = my interpretation. UNKNOWN = not verified. Fetched copies are in this folder (springer.md, springer2024.md, ind5.md, fluke.md, rw_wrench.md, rp_totals.md, rp_facts.md, mdpi_fa2.md, dv2.txt, pwe.md, reliamag_prod.md, rw_failuredata.md).

Headline: direct evidence on THIS exact question (conflicting alerts, what manager investigates next) is THIN. Nothing I found studies that decision in US mid-sized plants. What exists is indirect: small qualitative studies of PdM deployment, trade-press wrench-time benchmarks, one lab study on algorithm trust, and a lot of vendor material I could not use. Treat the weighing below as a reasoned lean, not a finding.

## Weighing the evidence

Lean (INFERRED, not proven): the preponderance of what I could verify points to organisation, planning capacity, trust and integration as at least co-equal blockers, and probably dominant for the decision "what do we do next once we know something is wrong". Information quality is real but acts mostly through trust.

For "information is the obstacle":
- False alarms and scarce failure data are acknowledged by authors and interviewees (Hermansa 2021; Hoffmann and Lasch 2025; MDPI review 2025).
- Conflicting black-box vendor outputs are hard to compare (Golightly 2018), which is the closest hit to your "conflicting reports" scenario.
- Records are often poor (RS/IMechE paper and Excel use; Reliabilityweb opinion).

For "permission/coordination/planning/trust is the obstacle":
- Peer-reviewed interview studies repeatedly find decision-fit, integration with scheduling, resourcing, reluctance, and trust matter more than analytic accuracy (Golightly 2018; behavioural PdM paper 2022; Hoffmann and Lasch 2025).
- Wrench-time literature (trade press) puts waiting for permits, access and parts as the bulk of lost technician time, so a better answer may not change when work happens.
- Workforce skills dominate stated obstacles in the Fluke survey, though the sponsor and wording issue weaken it. Deloitte/MI (independent) shows structural labour pressure.

Where the two are entangled: trust is partly caused by information quality. Early false alarms plus fast loss of confidence (Dietvorst 2015, plus experts in the 2022 paper) means information errors can become a coordination/trust problem that outlasts the fix. So an "info vs organisation" framing may be a false binary. A test that holds up: keep decision fit, alert precision and downtime approval separate in discovery questions.

Weight by source: peer-reviewed items are small N (6, 13, 15 interviews), mostly European, non-US, and expert-opinion based. The sampled surveys are vendor- or distributor-sponsored. I would not put quantitative weight on any single figure here.

## Gaps and conflicts

- No direct study of the target decision (which of several conflicting alerts to investigate next) in US mid-sized plants. Biggest gap.
- No independent, sampled alert-ignore or false-positive rate for manufacturing PdM. Every number I saw came from vendor pages (not opened) or algorithm papers.
- Wrench-time numbers conflict across the same trade outlet (18-30% vs 25-50%) and are mostly unsourced; Palmer's 35% is a single plant from 2007.
- Unverifiable circulating stats: 46% poor production communication (not in the Plant Engineering survey I opened), DOE 35% to 55-65% wrench time (not found in the DOE guide text I extracted), Aberdeen 3.5% vs 12%, 77.5% trust barrier (traced to a non-maintenance paper). Do not use these until the original is located.
- Fluke 78% figure is stated two different ways in the same article (share of respondents vs share of obstacles).
- Not opened: ScienceDirect study of 35 employee interviews at three organisations (access blocked; I only saw a search snippet), vendor pages, ASM/EEMUA benchmark documents, SMRP's own best-practice documents. Worth a follow-up because the 35-interview employee study is potentially the best qualitative source.
- Peer-reviewed interview studies are European and small; transfer to US mid-sized plants is INFERRED.
- Recommended next evidence: 5 to 8 customer discovery interviews with US plant maintenance managers and planners about the last time two reports disagreed (who decided, what was needed, who had to approve), which would reduce the main uncertainty cheaply.
